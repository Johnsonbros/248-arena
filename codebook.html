248 CMR 2.00 — Board, Licensing & Permits ▼

Administration

  • The Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters administers 248 CMR
  • Only a licensed Master Plumber can obtain a plumbing permit
  • A plumbing permit is valid for 1 year from date of issuance
  • Rough-in plumbing must be inspected before concealment of piping
  • A final inspection is required before a new system is placed in service

License Types

  • Apprentice: Registered with the Board; works under direct supervision of a Journeyman or Master
  • Journeyman: Requires 6,800 hours of licensed apprentice work experience plus 550 hours of tier education (five 110-hour tiers); passes the written exam; works under general supervision of a Master
  • Master: Experience as a Journeyman plus the Master exam; can pull permits, run a plumbing business, and supervise Journeymen and Apprentices
  • Licenses renew on a 2-year cycle with required continuing education

Permits

  • Permit required for: new fixtures, water heaters, water piping, gas piping
  • Permit NOT required for: replacing washers or flappers, clearing drain stoppages
  • Permitted work is performed by licensed plumbers (or registered apprentices under supervision)

The Exams (administered by PSI — closed book, computer-based, two parts)

  • Journeyman: Part I — 70 questions / 150 minutes; Part II — 30 questions / 120 minutes
  • Master: Part I — 60 questions / 120 minutes; Part II — 30 questions / 120 minutes
  • Passing score: 70% on each part
  • Questions are drawn from 248 CMR, MGL c. 142, and for gas NFPA 54 / NFPA 58 as amended — plus general trade practice
  • Topic areas: general regulations/inspections/permits · gas piping, equipment & appliances · venting · traps & cleanouts · fixtures, equipment & clearances · water heaters · piping, valves & controls · water supply · hangers & supports · drain, waste & vent · separators/interceptors/grease traps · joints & connections
  • Master eligibility: 1 year as a licensed Journeyman + 1,700 hours + Tier 5 (110 hrs)
  • Licenses renew every 2 years with 12 hours of continuing education
  • Confirm current requirements, fees, and the official content outline in the PSI candidate bulletin and at mass.gov before applying
248 CMR 10.03 — Definitions ▼

Key Definitions

  • Air Gap: The unobstructed vertical distance through free atmosphere between the lowest supply opening and the flood-level rim of the receptacle
  • Air Break: An indirect waste connection terminating below the receptor's flood rim but above the trap seal
  • Backflow: Flow into the potable distribution from any source other than the intended source — caused by backpressure or backsiphonage
  • Branch: Any part of the piping system other than a main, riser, or stack
  • Branch Interval: A stack section at least 8 feet high within which the horizontal branches of one story connect
  • Building Drain: The lowest piping receiving discharge inside the building, conveying it to the building sewer
  • Building Sewer: Horizontal piping from the building drain to the main sewer or point of disposal
  • Cross-Connection: Any connection between potable water and water of unknown or questionable safety
  • Fixture Unit (DFU): The load-producing value assigned to a fixture for drainage sizing
  • Flood-Level Rim: The edge of the receptacle from which water overflows
  • Potable Water: Water safe and satisfactory for drinking, cooking, and bathing
  • Stack Vent: The dry extension of a soil/waste stack above the highest horizontal branch
  • Trap: A fitting providing a liquid seal against sewer gas without materially affecting flow
  • Wet Vent: A pipe serving as a vent for one fixture and a drain for another
248 CMR 10.04 — Testing and Safety ▼

DWV Testing

  • Rough-in water test: fill to a minimum 10-foot head of water and hold with no loss
  • An approved air test may be used as an alternative
  • Testing happens before concealment and before the inspector's rough approval

Water Piping

  • Water distribution is tested at working pressure (or as the inspector requires) and proved tight
248 CMR 10.05 — General Regulations ▼
  • Used (secondhand) fixtures and materials may be reinstalled only if approved and restored to good, sanitary working condition
  • All work must be performed in a workmanlike manner and conform to 248 CMR
  • Piping must be protected from freezing, corrosion, and physical damage
248 CMR 10.06 — Materials ▼

Copper Tube Types

  • Type K (green) — heaviest wall; required for underground water service
  • Type L (blue) — general purpose water distribution
  • Type M (red) — thinnest standard wall; generally above-ground use inside buildings
  • DWV (yellow) — drainage/vent only

Drainage Materials

  • Approved underground: cast iron, PVC (Schedule 40), ABS
  • NOT approved underground: galvanized steel (corrosion)
  • PVC DWV conforms to ASTM D1785 (Sch 40) or ASTM D2665

PEX

  • Approved for water distribution per its listing
  • Keep at least 18 inches of metallic piping between PEX and a water heater connection
248 CMR 10.07 — Joints and Connections ▼
  • Only lead-free solder (≤0.2% lead) on potable water joints
  • PVC solvent joints require an approved primer before cement (ABS is cemented without primer)
  • Copper to galvanized steel requires a dielectric fitting (prevents galvanic corrosion)
  • No-hub cast iron joins with shielded couplings; hub-and-spigot uses gaskets or lead & oakum, hubs laid facing upstream
  • Threaded joints use tapered NPT threads with approved sealant
  • Concealed slip joints require an access panel for inspection
248 CMR 10.08 — Traps and Cleanouts ▼

Trap Rules

  • Water seal: not less than 2 inches, not more than 4 inches
  • S-traps are prohibited (self-siphoning); double trapping is prohibited
  • One trap per fixture — narrow exceptions such as adjacent compartments of a multi-compartment sink
  • Traps subject to evaporation need a trap seal primer or equivalent protection

Cleanouts

  • Required at every change of direction greater than 45°
  • Required at or near the base of every stack
  • Maximum spacing on horizontal drains: 75 feet
  • Size: same as the pipe served (up to 4"); 4" cleanout acceptable for larger pipes
  • Must remain accessible
248 CMR 10.09 — Interceptors, Separators & Holding Tanks ▼
  • Grease interceptors required for commercial kitchen fixtures discharging grease-laden waste
  • Food waste grinders must NOT discharge through a grease interceptor — they connect separately
  • Oil/sand separators serve garages and similar occupancies
  • Interceptors must be sized per the fixtures served and be accessible for cleaning

248 CMR 10.11 — Hangers and Supports ▼
  • Horizontal copper tube (1" and smaller): support at maximum 6-foot intervals
  • Cast iron: support at each joint / every 5 feet
  • Plastic piping requires closer support spacing than metal (typically 4 feet)
  • Vertical piping supported at each story
248 CMR 10.12 — Indirect Waste Piping ▼
  • Air gap: terminates above the receptor's flood rim (full separation)
  • Air break: terminates below the rim but above the trap seal
  • Commercial food-handling equipment discharges indirectly (air gap or air break) to a trapped receptor
  • Massachusetts requires a listed air gap fitting on dishwasher drains — a high loop alone is not acceptable
248 CMR 10.13 — Piping & Treatment of Special Wastes ▼
  • Corrosive chemical / laboratory waste requires approved acid-resistant piping
  • Special wastes are neutralized or treated before entering the sanitary drainage system
248 CMR 10.14 — Water Supply & Distribution ▼

Service & Pressure

  • Minimum water service size: 3/4 inch; minimum burial depth in MA: 48 inches
  • Water service vs. building sewer separation: 10 feet horizontal (or approved shelf installation)
  • Maximum static pressure at fixtures: 80 psi (PRV required above that)
  • Full-port shutoff at the service entrance; stop valve at each fixture supply; shutoff at each water heater
  • Water hammer arrestors where quick-closing valves are installed
  • Piping in exterior walls protected on the heated side of insulation

Hot Water

  • Residential fixtures: minimum 110°F; public handwashing max 110°F tempered
  • T&P relief valve: opens at 210°F / 150 psi; discharge full-size, unthreaded, terminating near the floor
  • Bottom-fed water heaters require a vacuum relief valve
  • Thermal expansion tank required on closed systems (backflow preventer on service)

Backflow Prevention

  • Backflow occurs by backpressure or backsiphonage
  • Air gap: simplest and most reliable — min 1 inch or 2× outlet diameter, whichever is greater
  • RPZ: high-hazard connections (boiler makeup, chemical feed); tested annually; min 12 inches above floor, never in a pit
  • DCVA: low-hazard (pollutant) connections only
  • AVB: min 6 inches above highest downstream outlet
  • Hose bibbs require a non-removable vacuum breaker; irrigation systems require an approved assembly

248 CMR 10.16 — Vents and Venting ▼

Purpose & Basics

  • Vents equalize pressure and protect trap seals from siphonage and back-pressure
  • Vent size: at least half the drain served, never under 1-1/4"; WC vent minimum 2 inches
  • Vent stack minimum: 2 inches; required for stacks of 5+ branch intervals
  • Relief vent at each 10th branch interval on stacks over 10 intervals
  • Vents graded to drain back by gravity; dry vents take off vertically above the drain centerline
  • No vent connection within 2 pipe diameters of the trap weir (no crown venting)
  • Vents over 40 ft developed length increase one pipe size
  • AAVs (air admittance valves) are generally prohibited in Massachusetts without specific Board approval

Roof Terminals

  • Extend at least 12 inches above the roof
  • Minimum 3 inches through the roof (frost closure) — any increase made ≥1 ft below the roof
  • Within 10 ft horizontally of a window/door/air intake: terminate at least 2 ft above the opening

Vent Types

  • Common vent: two same-level fixtures sharing one vent
  • Wet vent: vent for one fixture, drain for another — sized for the total load
  • Circuit vent: up to 8 fixtures in battery on a horizontal branch

  • Combined vent headers sized for the total DFU served
248 CMR 10.17 — Storm Drains ▼
  • Storm piping is sized by roof area and local rainfall rate — never by DFU
  • Storm water shall not discharge to the sanitary system (or to a septic system)
248 CMR 10.18 — Hospital Fixtures & Medical Gas ▼
  • Installation by ASSE 6010 certified personnel only
  • Type L (or K) copper, cleaned and capped for oxygen service (ASTM B819)
  • Joints brazed with BCuP alloy under continuous nitrogen purge — soldering prohibited
  • System verification by an independent ASSE 6030 verifier before use
  • U.S. color code: oxygen = green
248 CMR 3.00–7.00 — Gas Code Structure (what the exam draws from) ▼

How Massachusetts organizes fuel gas

  • 248 CMR 3.00 — General provisions governing the conduct of plumbing and gas work (licensing conduct, business/apprentice licenses, inspector requirements)
  • 248 CMR 4.00 — The Massachusetts Fuel Gas Code — the umbrella gas regulation
  • 248 CMR 5.00 — Massachusetts amendments to NFPA 54 (National Fuel Gas Code)
  • 248 CMR 6.00 — Massachusetts amendments to NFPA 58 (Liquefied Petroleum Gas Code)
  • 248 CMR 7.00 — Large gas utilization equipment
  • 248 CMR 10.00 — Uniform State Plumbing Code · 248 CMR 11.00 — Education & experience standards

Why it matters: in Massachusetts, gas fitting is licensed together with plumbing — the journeyman exam tests both. Gas questions come from 248 CMR 4.00–7.00 plus NFPA 54/58 as amended by Massachusetts, so a national NFPA answer can be wrong here. Always follow the MA amendment.

248 CMR 4.00–7.00 — Fuel Gas Practice (NFPA 54/58 as amended by MA) ▼

General Rules

  • Minimum gas pipe size meter → appliance: 1/2 inch
  • Max pressure drop (low-pressure system): 0.5 inch WC
  • Sediment trap (drip leg) at every appliance inlet
  • Shutoff valve at the meter and within 6 feet of each appliance
  • Sizing based on BTU demand, run length, pressure drop, and specific gravity
  • Appliances in bedrooms/bathrooms must be direct-vent (sealed combustion)
  • Confined spaces need two combustion-air openings (top and bottom)

Materials & Installation

  • Black iron: threaded or welded; approved gas-rated thread compound only
  • CSST must be bonded to the grounding system
  • PE plastic: underground outdoors only, with tracer wire and approved riser
  • Underground burial: minimum 12 inches of cover
  • Through masonry: sleeved; never through ducts, chimneys, or elevator shafts
  • Flexible appliance connectors: max 6 feet, never through walls or floors
  • Regulator vents terminate outdoors, protected, away from openings

Testing & Safety

  • New piping test: 3 psi for 10 minutes, no perceptible drop (air/inert gas only)
  • Leak checks with solution or gauge — never a flame
  • LP gas is heavier than air and pools in low areas

Venting Appliances (Categories I–IV)

  • Category I — negative vent pressure, non-condensing (atmospheric furnaces, most water heaters): vented with B-vent or masonry chimney
  • Category III — positive pressure, non-condensing: requires listed, gas-tight special vent
  • Category IV — positive pressure, condensing (high-efficiency): vented with listed plastic (PVC/CPVC/PP) per the manufacturer, with a condensate drain
  • Vent connectors slope upward at least 1/4 inch per foot toward the vent
  • Never connect an appliance to a vent serving a solid-fuel appliance

Combustion Air (NFPA 54 as amended)

  • Standard method for a confined space using indoor air: 1 square inch per 1,000 BTU/hr per opening, minimum 100 sq in each, one high and one low
  • Using outdoor air through vertical ducts: 1 sq in per 4,000 BTU/hr; horizontal ducts: 1 sq in per 2,000 BTU/hr
  • An unconfined space has at least 50 cubic feet per 1,000 BTU/hr of total input

LP Gas (NFPA 58 as amended — 248 CMR 6.00)

  • LP is heavier than air (vapor density ~1.5) — it settles; natural gas rises
  • Approx. 2,500 BTU per cubic foot (vs ~1,000 for natural gas); ~91,500 BTU per gallon
  • Typical appliance manifold pressure: 11 in WC for LP vs 3.5 in WC for natural gas
  • Container placement, clearances to openings/ignition sources, and filling limits are set by NFPA 58 as amended
  • Never install LP appliances or containers in basements/pits where vapor can accumulate

Massachusetts-Specific Gas Notes

  • Gas fitting is licensed with plumbing in MA — the journeyman exam covers both
  • MA amendments in 248 CMR 5.00/6.00 override the base NFPA text — answer to the amendment
  • 248 CMR 7.00 governs large gas utilization equipment
  • Gas permits and inspections follow the same Board/inspector process as plumbing
